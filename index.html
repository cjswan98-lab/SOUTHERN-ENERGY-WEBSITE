A capital-efficient model for African energy.

We identify distressed or under-used infrastructure and deploy targeted capital to restore it, and where the opportunity calls for it we build new capacity from the ground up. We keep what we build, carry the operating risk and collect the revenue.

Southern Energy is the investment vehicle of VerdAfrique Holding, a Mauritius-based renewable energy holding company. It holds controlling stakes in the project companies that own and operate each plant, and partners with local shareholders, state entities and technical co-developers in each country.

Power is sold in US dollars across our operating projects, giving hard-currency income insulated from local-currency swings, and our direct-to-consumer model secures long-term demand for every unit we generate. We refurbish proven infrastructure first, delivering capacity at a fraction of new-build cost with hydrology and grid connections already in place, then expand with new solar, storage and thermal capacity.

Our Congolese operations are carried out through Southern Energy RDC SARLU in Kinshasa and Greentech Energy S.A. in Bunia, which holds the Budana hydro plant and a 30-year electricity distribution concession for the city.

In Zimbabwe, Makomo Resources is the largest independent coal supplier to ZETDC and the fuel base for the Hwange thermal power project.

Across the group we combine three things that are rarely found together in frontier markets: an operating utility with real customers and cash flow, a construction pipeline with credible EPC partners, and a development team that has taken projects from concept through permitting, power purchase agreements and financial close.

Operating

Bunia Hydro

Budana hydropower plant
Ituri Province, DRC

Budana Power Station sits on the River Shari above Bunia, a city of up to two million people and the capital of Ituri Province. Its dam, penstocks and powerhouse were built decades ago and had fallen into serious disrepair: when the group arrived in 2024 one Francis turbine was barely generating under 1 MW and the city's grid was largely non-functional.

Both turbines were overhauled on site to their rated 2.45 MW and synchronised in September 2025, delivering 4.9 MW of continuous baseload at around 98% uptime. The grid was rebuilt from the ground up — new cables, restored poles, step-up and step-down transformers — and more than 5,000 prepaid smart meters now bill customers in US dollars, so every unit delivered generates revenue. Greentech Energy holds the exclusive concession to refurbish, operate and sell power from the site, in a 60/40 joint venture with SOKIMO, the DRC state gold-mining company.

Before grid power, Bunia's shops, hospitals and homes ran on expensive diesel. Today hospitals run diagnostic equipment, supermarkets keep refrigerated stock, streetlights run through the night and commerce extends past dark, at a fraction of what diesel cost.

  • 1Two turbines online, full grid rebuild, 5,000+ prepaid meters — 4.9 MW, September 2025, at roughly a third of new-build cost
  • 2Turbine 3 refurbishment (6.5 MW) — late 2026, taking the plant to 11.4 MW
  • 3Solar hybrid + storage — 8 MW solar and 32 MWh of batteries by 2028, 19.4 MW peak capacity
Under construction

Victoria Falls Solar

Power Ventures Zimbabwe
Matabeleland North, Zimbabwe

Power Ventures is building the Victoria Falls Solar Park, a utility-scale PV plant near Victoria Falls Airport, licensed for 100 MW and built in three tranches to full capacity by 2027/28. All material permits are in place: the generation licence was issued in August 2019, environmental approvals are secured and a long-term land lease is signed. The plant connects to the Hwange–Victoria Falls 88 kV line through a loop-in less than half a kilometre from site.

The site sits in one of the world's strongest solar corridors, receiving 7,735 MJ/m² a year — roughly 2,150 peak sun hours and more than double the UK's resource. Zimbabwe needs about 2,200 MW against domestic generation of around 1,300 MW, and industrial customers run diesel at significant cost. Selling to them in US dollars gives durable savings against self-generation, and Phase 3 opens regional export through the Southern African Power Pool.

  • 1Solar I — 5 MW, first generation expected September 2026; substation funded by the EPC contractor
  • 2Solar II — +20 MW in 2026/27, taking installed capacity to 25 MW
  • 3Solar III — +75 MW in 2027/28, delivering the full 100 MW licensed capacity

Development projects

Beyond the three active projects, the group is building out a portfolio of thermal, hydro and hybrid projects across Zimbabwe and the DRC — from a national-status thermal station at Hwange to hydro rehabilitations with SNEL.

The Congo basin holds some of the world's greatest untapped hydropower potential, yet only a fraction of the country's historic generating stations still run. Hundreds lie dormant with dams, intakes and penstocks largely intact. The barrier is operational, not hydrological — and the restoration model proven at Budana transfers directly to those sites.

Our development model is the same everywhere: a joint venture with the national utility or a state partner, private-sector financing, prepaid-meter revenue security, and long-term concession management covering the full value chain from generation to billing. Bunia is the operating proof of that model.

In the DRC we have completed technical due diligence, signed MOUs, engaged SNEL at leadership level and brought DFI financing partners to sites identified across Ituri, Tanganyika and Kasaï provinces. In Ituri, a proposed partnership with the Gouvernorat covers electricity, potable water and roads, with Budana 2 as its first project.

Why it matters

Our plants replace diesel generation and imported power in places where the alternative is no power at all. Every megawatt expands energy access and displaces diesel, advancing UN Sustainable Development Goals 7, 8 and 13.

Firm, affordable power

Hydro and thermal provide baseload; solar and storage add daytime capacity and evening cover. Every kilowatt-hour displaces diesel that would otherwise be trucked in.

Local economies

Reliable electricity lets hospitals, universities, banks and data centres operate in Bunia. Construction and operations create skilled jobs in each host community.

Public infrastructure

From airport electrification to a proposed potable-water network, the group's utilities are built to serve the public institutions of the provinces they operate in.
